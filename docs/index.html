A measured baseline for Pakistani statute retrieval

Eight commercial legal AI products serve Pakistan. Every one advertises an accuracy figure. None publishes evidence.

When the same claims were tested in the United States, the largest incumbents were found to fabricate between 17% and 33% of the time. No equivalent test has ever been run on Pakistani law.

This is an attempt at the first honest number, with the method published so you can check it.

Not yet a finding

These numbers run against a 30-provision demo corpus, not the real statute text. They demonstrate the pipeline works. Real figures require the full corpus and the 619-pair LEGAL-UQA evaluation set.

What the numbers already show

Adding a regex channel for explicit section references lifts recall@1 from 0.852 to 0.963. That reproduces and then fixes a documented failure mode: in a published audit of a legal retrieval system, 31% of failures were requests for a specific article reference, because embedding models rank thematically-similar provisions above the one actually named.

To an embedding model, Section 302 and Section 320 occupy nearly the same point in vector space. To a keyword index they are unrelated tokens. Pakistani legal queries are full of exactly these strings.
